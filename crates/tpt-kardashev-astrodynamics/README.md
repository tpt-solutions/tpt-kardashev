# tpt-kardashev-astrodynamics

Time scales, reference frames, orbital elements, propagation, Lambert solving and impulsive manoeuvres.

**Status:** early slice. Implemented: unit newtypes (`Kilometres`, `Seconds`, `Radians`), a small `Vec3`, Keplerian elements for elliptic two-body orbits with conversions to and from state vectors, epochs on the TT scale with TAI, UTC (through a caller-supplied leap-second table) and Julian dates, the perifocal and RTN frames, two-body propagation by universal variables (`propagate::propagate`, which also covers hyperbolic states), and numerical propagation: a `ForceModel` trait, central-body gravity (`force::CentralBody`), a J2 zonal perturbation (`force::J2Zonal`, with WGS 84 constants), zonal harmonics of any degree with caller-supplied coefficients (`force::ZonalHarmonics`), a fixed-step RK4 `integrate::Cowell` propagator, a velocity Verlet `symplectic::Verlet` propagator for position-only forces, impulsive manoeuvres (`manoeuvre`: Hohmann, bi-elliptic, and plane change), Lambert's problem (`lambert::lambert`, zero-revolution transfers), a low-precision analytic ephemeris for the Sun and Moon (`ephemeris`), third-body gravity (`force::ThirdBody`), solar radiation pressure with the Earth's umbra and penumbra (`force::SolarRadiationPressure`, `force::lit_fraction`), atmospheric drag from an exponential atmosphere (`force::AtmosphericDrag`, `atmosphere`), finite burns (`force::Thrust`), ground-station visibility (`visibility`: WGS 84 stations, elevation, and contact windows), an SPK kernel reader (`spk`), and near-earth SGP4 with two-line element parsing (`sgp4`). Everything else in the v0.1 scope is not written yet (see the workspace `todo.md` §2.2).

## Features

- `std` (default): links the standard library. The element conversions need it, because `core` has no trigonometry.
- `alloc`: enables heap-allocating APIs. Core is `no_std`.

## Conventions

- Positions are in km, velocities in km/s, and the gravitational parameter μ in km³/s². Each function takes μ explicitly; the crate bundles no body constants.
- Angles are in radians, returned in `[0, 2π)`. The reference plane is the x–y plane, and `h = r × v` is the orbit normal.
- Circular and equatorial orbits use fixed conventions, so each state has one element set: circular orbits take ω = 0 and ν as the argument of latitude; equatorial orbits take Ω = 0 and ω as the longitude of periapsis; circular equatorial orbits take ν as the true longitude. Retrograde orbits measure angles in the sense of the orbit.

## Optional features

- `sgp4` (default): SGP4/SDP4 and TLE parsing (`sgp4`, `sdp4`). Implies `std`. Without it the crate has the frames, elements, force models, integrators and Lambert solver, but no TLE propagation.
- No `relativity` feature. The spec's edge to `tpt-kardashev-relativity` is not taken, because CLAUDE.md forbids repo-internal dependencies (decided 2026-11-18, see `todo.md`). The TDB periodic series is in `tdb`.

## What it does not model

- Parabolic and hyperbolic orbits. Their states are rejected with `Error::NotElliptic`.
- Equinoctial elements are written (`equinoctial::Equinoctial`) and regular for circular and equatorial orbits, but the Keplerian set keeps the fixed conventions above for those cases. Equinoctial elements are not yet used for the propagator's inputs.
- TDB, UT1 and Earth orientation. Leap seconds come from the caller's table, which the crate does not bundle; a UTC second of 60 (leap-second insertion) is rejected. Epochs are `f64` seconds past J2000, resolving about 0.5 µs.
- Visibility has no atmospheric refraction, no terrain masking, and no station motion. Its Earth rotation starts from an angle the caller gives at the first instant, so it does not use UT1 or sidereal time. Contact windows are found by sampling at a fixed step, so a pass shorter than the step can be missed. Reference frames beyond perifocal, RTN and LVLH. LVLH (`frames::lvlh_matrix`) uses one convention, chosen for this crate and documented there: x along-track, y along the negative orbit normal, z toward nadir. Other users order or sign the axes differently. The heliocentric ecliptic frame is written as a conversion from the Earth-centred equatorial frame (`frames::heliocentric_ecliptic`), using the low-precision Sun. The Earth-fixed position transform is written (`frames::inertial_to_earth_fixed`), but it takes the Earth rotation angle as an input. Computing that angle needs UT1, which the crate does not bundle, and the Earth-fixed velocity is not transformed. The inertial frame used throughout is the mean equator and equinox of J2000; it is not the GCRF, which differs by the frame bias (about 0.02 arcseconds), and that difference is not modelled. Force models beyond central gravity, J2, the Sun and Moon as third bodies, solar pressure, drag and thrust (bundled J3 and higher constants: `force::ZonalHarmonics` takes its coefficients from the caller). A thrust has a constant acceleration: the spacecraft's mass is not tracked, so there is no mass flow or propellant use. Its edges are discontinuous, so a fixed step should be much shorter than the burn (see `force::Thrust`). The drag uses an exponential atmosphere with no solar-cycle dependence, and returns zero above 1000 km, which understates drag at the top of the table's range; the solar pressure has no albedo or infrared term, and its penumbra assumes a spherical Earth and a Sun of the IAU 2015 nominal radius; J2 is given in an inertial frame with the spin axis as z, so the Earth's rotation is not applied, multi-revolution Lambert transfers, and ephemerides beyond the Sun and Moon. The series are low precision: the Sun to about 0.01° in longitude, the Moon to a few tenths of a degree. Neither is a bundled ephemeris. `spk` reads a caller's SPK kernel (type 2 only, J2000 frame, little-endian) and has been tested only on kernels built in the test suite: no real kernel is checked, and none is bundled. Its epochs are seconds past J2000 on the TDB scale, not converted from TT. It does not chain segments, so each body needs a segment relative to the centre it is asked for. The ephemeris is in the J2000 ecliptic, so the third-body force rotates it into the equatorial frame, where J2 and the spacecraft state are defined. Lambert transfers whose plane contains the z axis are rejected, since their sense cannot be read from the z component of the angular momentum. The manoeuvres are instantaneous and two-body; the plane change is a single burn, with no combined apsidal plane change beyond the law of cosines. The propagator does not report events such as periapsis passage. Integration comes in three forms: the fixed-step RK4 `integrate::Cowell`, the adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) `adaptive::AdaptiveCowell`, which controls the local error with a relative and an absolute tolerance, and the fixed-step velocity Verlet `symplectic::Verlet`. The adaptive form has no event location and no dense output. Verlet is valid only for accelerations that do not depend on velocity, and the crate cannot check that: drag and finite thrust read velocity, so use the RK4 or adaptive forms for them. Verlet has no error control and integrates one state, not several bodies at once. `sgp4` covers near-earth orbits (period under 225 minutes) and deep-space orbits through SDP4 (`sdp4`: lunar-solar terms and the one-day and half-day resonances). Its resonance integrator starts from the epoch on each call, so it does not carry state between calls. It uses the WGS-72 constants by default, as the published test cases do, and gives states in the TEME frame, in km and km/s, relative to the element epoch. Element-set checksums are not verified. Twenty-seven cases of the published verification set with printed outputs are tested: seven near-earth ones covering the drag branches (`tests/sgp4*.rs`, including the original Report #3 case 88888), and twenty deep-space ones (`tests/sdp4.rs`, `tests/sdp4_more.rs`). Most agree with the printed states to about 5×10⁻⁹ km. Five agree to 2×10⁻⁶ to 1.7×10⁻⁵ km, and 08195 to 2.3×10⁻⁴ km at 2400 minutes; these residuals are not yet explained, so they have wider bounds. The model is behind the `sgp4` feature, which is on by default and implies `std`. Force-model limits are stated in full at v0.1 (todo §2.2).
- No ephemeris data is bundled. Low-thrust: Edelbaum's approximation for a circular-to-circular transfer with a plane change (`low_thrust::edelbaum`) is written; its limits are stated in that module, and it is not an optimal-steering solution. SGP4 stays behind the `sgp4` feature (spec §12.4).

## Checks

- Known values: a geostationary-radius circular equatorial orbit (a = r, e = 0, i = 0), and an inclined circular orbit (i = 0.5 rad, Ω = 0).
- Invariants: for an elliptic state, |h| = √(μ·a(1 − e²)) and the energy is −μ/(2a).
- Round trips: state → elements → state over a grid of 2160 element sets, including circular, equatorial, polar and retrograde cases; and elements → state → elements away from the singular cases, with the elements recovered.
- Rejection: hyperbolic and radial states, non-finite values, and non-positive μ.
- Long arcs: the angular-momentum vector over twenty orbits under the adaptive integrator drifts by 1.7×10⁻¹¹ relative. Velocity Verlet keeps energy within a bounded band over a thousand orbits (its per-orbit peak unchanged between the first and last hundred), and conserves angular momentum to 1.5×10⁻¹⁴ over a hundred orbits. RK4 at the same step grows tenfold over the same arc.
- Time: J2000 is Julian date 2451545.0 TT; TAI is TT − 32.184 s; 2000-01-01 12:00 UTC is 64.184 s before the J2000 instant in TT; the TAI difference across the 2017 leap second is 2 s; UTC round trips through TT; and invalid calendar values are rejected. The leap-second fixture is a test table, not crate data.
