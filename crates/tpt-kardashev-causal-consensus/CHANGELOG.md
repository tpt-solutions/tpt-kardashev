# Changelog: tpt-kardashev-causal-consensus

## Unreleased

- `clock::HybridClock` and `clock::Timestamp`: hybrid logical clock. Timestamps are strictly increasing per node, strictly after any remote timestamp received, and a remote timestamp more than the configured drift ahead of local physical time is rejected without changing the clock. Tests in `tests/clocks.rs` (10 000 steps with physical time jumping and going backwards, causality along a three-node message chain, rejection leaves the clock unchanged, the wall part stays within the largest reading plus the bound across three nodes) and a unit test of counter overflow in `src/clock.rs`.
- `vector::VectorClock<N>` and `vector::CausalRelation`: fixed-size vector clock with tick, merge (entry-wise maximum) and the four relations. Tests in `tests/clocks.rs`: each relation, merge commutativity and idempotence, and an out-of-range index.
- `error::ClockError` (`#[non_exhaustive]`): `DriftExceeded`, `CounterOverflow`, `NodeOutOfRange`.
- `consistency::ConsistencyLevel` and `consistency::Delays`: the four levels from the spec, with the cross-domain and domain round-trip classification and a latency lower bound per level from the one-way link delays. Zero for Commutative and Causal; two intra-domain one-way delays for DomainLinearizable; two cross-domain one-way delays for GlobalAgreed. Tests in `tests/consistency.rs`.
- `causal::CausalBroadcast` and `causal::Envelope` (feature `alloc`): causal broadcast by vector-clock dependencies. A message is delivered when the receiver has delivered its causes; duplicates are dropped, including a second copy of a buffered message. Tests in `tests/causal_broadcast.rs`: a reply waits for its cause; a duplicate is delivered once; a duplicate of a waiting message leaves nothing behind; and a seeded simulation of four nodes with random sends, reordering and duplicates, where every node delivers every message once and in causal order. A duplicate-while-waiting bug was found by this test and fixed.
- `transport::Transport<M>` (feature `alloc`): a message transport trait whose time is passed in by the caller. `transport::SimNetwork<M>` is the deterministic simulation: seeded delay and jitter (jitter reorders), loss, duplication, and timed partitions of a pair of nodes in both directions, with counters. Deliveries come in order of time, ties in send order, through a `BTreeMap`, so the order never depends on hashing. Tests in `tests/transport.rs`: the delay, reordering, loss and duplication shares, partitions that cut and heal, a seed that gives the same run, and rejection of invalid configuration.
- `domain::RaftNode` (feature `alloc`): leader election with seeded randomised timeouts, heartbeats, log replication with conflict repair, and commitment by majority in the leader's term. A pure state machine: `tick`, `handle` and `propose` return the messages to send. Tests in `tests/domain.rs` on a five-node simulated cluster: one leader is elected; a command commits in order on every node; a follower refuses a command and names the leader; an isolated leader cannot commit, the majority elects a new leader and commits, and the old leader's command is never committed after the heal; and safety (one leader per term, agreement on committed entries, commit never goes backwards) is checked at every step under loss, duplication and partitions. Membership change and log compaction are not written.
- `hash::sha256` and `hash::Sha256` (streaming, no heap): SHA-256 per FIPS 180-4. The round constants are derived from the cube roots of the first 64 primes and the initial state from the square roots of the first 8, with exact integer roots. Tests in `tests/hash.rs`: the published digests for the empty message, `abc` and the 448-bit message, and streaming in every split of several lengths.
- `federation`: hash-chained epoch summaries (`EpochSummary`, `SummaryChain`, `verify_chain`, `digest_commands`, `GENESIS`) (feature `alloc`). Each summary names the previous summary's hash and a digest of the epoch's commands. Tests in `tests/federation.rs`: an intact chain verifies to its head; a changed summary, a reordering, a dropped summary and a summary from another domain are each detected. Signatures are not written.
- `domain::RaftNode` majority uses `div_ceil`; no behaviour change.
- `transport::SimNetwork::partition_sets` and `crash`: cut every link between two node groups for a window, and take one node's traffic down for a window. Tests in `tests/transport.rs`.
- `linearizable::is_linearizable`, `Op`, `Event`, `LinError`, `MAX_OPERATIONS` (feature `alloc`): linearizability of a single-register history, by depth-first search with memoised failed states, for up to 16 operations. Tests in `tests/linearizable.rs`: sequential, stale, concurrent and order-disagreeing histories, a never-written value, invalid intervals, and the size limit.
- `global::Domain<Op>`, `global::Proposal<Op>`, `global::Proposer`, `global::GlobalMessage<Op>`, `global::Outcome` (feature `alloc`): `GlobalAgreed` operations. Each domain accepts the first operation it sees for a key and rejects a different one; a proposal commits once a majority of domains accept it, so two conflicting values for one key cannot both commit. Each domain seals its accepted proposals into a hash-chained summary. Tests in `tests/global.rs`: the commit latency is exactly one round trip and never below the `ConsistencyLevel` bound; no key commits two values over 40 seeds with jitter (with checks that commits actually happen); different keys both commit; a minority of two of five cannot commit while the majority of three does; and every domain's sealed chain verifies. Single round, no crash recovery, no Byzantine case.
